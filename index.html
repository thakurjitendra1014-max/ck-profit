GST at this price

GST is not one of your costs — you collect it from the customer and hand it over. Output GST is the tax sitting inside the price the customer paid. Input GST is the tax you already paid inside your purchase, Amazon's fees, packaging and ads; because you are GST registered you claim that back as input tax credit. You deposit only the difference, the net GST. So a higher price does not just move your profit — above the ₹2,500 line the product rate goes from 5% to 18%, and the Net GST column in the ladder shows exactly how much more you start depositing. A price in the 5% band often collects less GST than you have already paid on the fees and the piece itself; then there is nothing to deposit and the leftover credit carries forward to the next sale, which is why the figure can read as credit instead. Amazon separately deducts TCS on your sales and deposits it against your GSTIN, which reduces what you pay in cash when you file; the exact figure is in your GST portal ledger, not here.

Package calculator

Couriers bill you on chargeable weight: the higher of what the parcel actually weighs and its volumetric weight, which is L×B×H in cm divided by 5000. A light, bulky comforter is almost always billed on volume, so the box size decides the cost, not the product. Amazon then rounds the chargeable weight up to the next half kilo and charges the slab rate from Settings. Dimensions below start as a working-back from the shipping weight on file — measure your real box and correct them. Measure in whichever unit suits you — everything is converted to centimetres underneath, because that is the unit Amazon's formula uses.

How profit is calculated

Five steps, in the same order as the Cost breakdown.
  1. Start with what the customer pays — your selling price, minus any coupon.
  2. Take out product GST — 5% up to ₹2,500, 18% above it. This money is collected for the government, so it was never yours. What is left is your net revenue.
  3. Take out the purchase cost — what you pay your supplier for the piece.
  4. Take out the marketplace's charges — on Amazon: referral fee, closing fee, weight handling, plus pick & pack and storage on FBA. On Flipkart: commission (0% up to the band limit for your verticals), the fixed fee that also covers delivery, pick & pack on FBF, and an allowance for customer returns, because each return costs you the reverse pickup.
  5. Take out your own costs — packaging, advertising, a returns allowance, and anything you added under Other expenses.
Whatever is left is your profit per unit. Margin is that profit divided by the selling price.
The finer points.
• GST is judged on the price after the coupon, so a ₹2,600 price with a ₹200 coupon stays in the 5% band.
• Amazon and Flipkart charge 18% GST on top of all their own fees. With ITC on, that 18% is left out of the costs here because you claim it back as credit — same for the supplier's 5% on your purchase cost. Turn ITC off in Settings and everything is counted with its GST. The Ladder tab shows the whole GST picture for any price.
• Shipping is an estimate from chargeable weight — the higher of actual weight and volumetric (L×B×H cm ÷ 5000) — using the weight slabs in Settings, scaled by the calibration factor. Whenever you have the real quote, type it straight into the shipping line on the Cost breakdown.
• On your own website, RTO loss assumes a failed order costs you shipping both ways plus the packaging.
